# Fixed-bore clarinet experiments

## Two fingerings

`fixed-bore.muz` uses one nonlinear reed with three bidirectional delay
segments and two junctions. The register vent is a filtered local pressure
release. The G tone hole blends the local scattering between bore
transmission and reflection from an open end. The experiment has two
fingerings, concert A4 and F4, and tests the register transition with a
fixed bore:

```sh
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/register-slurs.muz \
  -o out/register-slurs.wav
```

## Tone-hole lattice clarinet

`clarinet_bore.muz` is a fixed-geometry B-flat clarinet, written E3–F6
(sounding MIDI 50–87). `lattice-clarinet.muz` plays it with the
`std/woodwinds` player's breath and articulation and a player model of its
own. Its parts:

- **Bore.** Cylinder segments between 20 holes (17 tone holes, two ring
  vents and the register hole) and a bell, as cubic delay lines, with a
  loss shelf on the mouthpiece segment and a two-pole bell reflection. Wall
  loss is lumped onto every fifth segment (each segment loses under 0.5%).
  Every segment stays live: an open hole does not cut off the bore below
  it. The bore may change radius: the top segment is the mouthpiece's
  chamber, the main bore steps at three holes (`t`, `cs`, `gsds`: the upper
  joint's reverse taper and the lower joint's widening, as a polycylinder),
  and the bell may flare through short cylinders of growing radius. Each
  step scatters with `(S_above - S_below) / (S_above + S_below)`, a hole at
  a step sees the junction's mean area, and the bell radiates its volume
  flow. The committed geometry keeps all of these at the bore's radius
  (see the limits below).
- **Holes.** Each hole is a shunt junction with pressure
  `p = (1 - H_open)(1 - H_closed)(a + b)`. The open path is an inertance
  with a viscous resistance (narrower holes are lossier), a one-pole
  release whose corner and depth follow the open path's admittance
  `g(x)` at the key's travel. A pad and a finger over its hole both act as
  a curtain, `g = (1 + q) x / (x + q)`, so most of the change happens near
  the seat. The closed chimney is a compliance. The register hole also has
  a jet resistance growing with its flow (Atig, Dalmont & Gilbert 2004;
  Dalmont et al. 2002). It goes as the hole's area squared: the fitted tube
  is an effective 1.32 mm in radius against the real tube's 1.47–1.5 mm
  (YCL-250; Buffet Festival, Colinot et al. 2026), and C = 0.36 on it is
  the jet that the literature's C = 0.6–0.7 gives the real tube. The tool
  can fit the register tube as the real one, 12–14 mm long and projecting
  into the bore (an unflanged inner end, its wall displacing bore volume).
- **Geometry.** `clarinet_geometry.muz` is generated by
  `tools/clarinet-lattice.py fit` from `clarinet-geometry.json`. The tool
  fits hole positions, radii, chimneys and losses to the measured
  impedance peaks of a real B-flat clarinet, UNSW's `ImpedClarinet.xlsx`
  (Dickens, Smith & Wolfe), reduced to `clarinet-reference.json`. Its
  frequency-domain model is the discrete-time system the renderer runs
  (third-order Lagrange delays, prewarped trapezoidal one-poles at
  96 kHz), so the rendered lattice matches the fit. Two ring vents follow
  a measured Yamaha YCL-250 (Moers & Kergomard 2011): `v`, open only with
  the thumb and LH1 both up (open G), and the sliver `sl` above LH2. The
  result is an effective geometry, not a drawing. The fit weighs the
  alternates double, can weigh the third peaks (`--third`), holds the
  throat notes as played (right hand down) near the measured peaks, and
  with `--stage profile` or `all` fits the bore's profile too.
- **Fingerings.** Each written note maps to its open holes, checked
  against UNSW's fingering diagrams and Boehm charts: chalumeau, the
  clarion a twelfth up with the register key, the altissimo on the fifth
  harmonic with LH1 vented (and the G#/D# key from D6 up). The throat notes
  are played with the right hand down, as players do to cross the break.
  Alternates (`fingering` control): the forked Eb4/Bb5 and the throat Bb4
  with the A key and side trill key. Quarter tones shade the changing holes
  to the fraction `shade` that sounds between the semitones
  (`tools/clarinet-lattice.py quarters` folds the played error into it).
  Across the break and into the altissimo the fingering changes just below
  the quarter tone and the player bends the upper fingering down.
- **Keys.** A finger or pad leaves its stop at rest and lands on its seat
  or stop at speed: the travel is a slew toward a target past either end,
  read through curves that hold their end values, so each motion ends at a
  definite moment and its filters rest. A finger moves in 5 ms and a spring
  in 8 ms from the note's change (Almeida, Chow, Smith & Wolfe 2009
  measured about 10 and 16 ms whole motions on the flute; the note's
  change already includes the player's anticipation). Fingers arrive in a
  fixed order (thumb and register key, left hand, right hand, little
  fingers), `stagger` times the note's `key_time` (0–1, default 0.4)
  apart. A note's `vent` expression (0–1) lifts the register key by that
  much of its travel on top of the fingering; `lc.vented(rule)` is a note
  rule for `w.perform` or `w.play` that writes it over the last
  `vent_lead` (35 ms) before a slur up across the break, as players lead
  with the thumb and register key (`lattice-slurs.muz` uses it).
- **Reed and lip.** The reed is `reed_dynamics.dynamic`, second order,
  its flow solved in closed form. One lip force sets it, in newtons over
  `lip_full` (1.75 N, estimated) as F (0–1): a firmer
  lip closes the rest opening H and stiffens the reed K, so its closing
  pressure KH rises, zeta (as sqrt(H/K)) falls, and its resonance
  (1.5–2.7 kHz) and damping (q 0.15–0.45) rise. These ranges follow
  Tonetti & Piana (2025, reed resonance and damping against lip load),
  Almeida et al. (2013, squeaks near 1.0–1.2 kHz bound the lip-loaded
  resonance) and Szwarcberg et al. (q at least 0.2, zeta 0.05–0.4). The
  force at mf is 1.4 N in the chalumeau and throat, the middle of the G3
  and G4 playing regions Almeida, George, Smith & Wolfe (2013) measured
  with a lip at the middle of the reed. They measured G5 at 0.4–0.9 N; this
  reed holds the clarion's forte (F#5, B4) only from about 1.1 N, so the
  clarion's 1.14 N, like the altissimo's 1.4 N, is estimated. It falls
  0.1 N per kPa of blowing pressure as the player gets louder (players
  loosen to play louder; Almeida et al.'s contours of equal pitch fall
  0.3 N per kPa, and a player who loosened that much would break this
  reed's forte clarion). The reed and the vocal tract are in units of the
  mouthpiece chamber's characteristic impedance.
- **Breath.** Blowing pressure falls about 8% per octave up the range and
  rises 15% on the lowest clarion notes, after Fuks & Sundberg's measured
  arpeggios. Breath turbulence is a noise flow at the reed channel, so it
  radiates through the bore.
- **Vocal tract.** One resonance upstream of the reed, about 1 bore
  characteristic impedance in the chalumeau rising to 6 in the upper
  clarion and 10 in the altissimo, 150 Hz (altissimo 120 Hz) above the
  note but not below 600 Hz (Chen, Smith & Wolfe 2009; Pàmies-Vilà et al.
  2020 found tract adjustments in a fraction of clarion notes and most
  altissimo ones). On a slur up into a higher register, and on a tongued
  attack in the clarion or altissimo, the player voices the new note: for
  40 ms the resonance moves onto it, 10 stronger. The `voicing` control adds
  up to 8.
- **Tuning.** The player keeps the note in tune with a variable mouthpiece
  length, from the tuned frequency against the fingering's own pitch at
  the current dynamic (`tools/clarinet-lattice.py tune`, with two passes
  that fold the residual into `clarinet_tuning.muz`), between 70 cents down
  and 50 up.
- **Radiation.** Each open hole and the bell radiate as monopoles: the
  far-field pressure is the time derivative of their summed volume flow
  (an open hole's flow is twice its pressure release; the bell's the
  incident less the reflected wave). Their spacing and the bell's
  directivity are left out.

Render the slurs, a phrase, the phrase on both clarinets, and the range
with alternates, quarter tones and a bend:

```sh
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/lattice-slurs.muz -o out/lattice-slurs.wav
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/lattice-phrase.muz -o out/lattice-phrase.wav
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/blend-phrase.muz -o out/blend-phrase.wav
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/lattice-fingerings.muz -o out/lattice-fingerings.wav
```

### Measurements (2026-11-14)

- **Passive impedance.** The fit against the reference over the 38
  standard fingerings: first peak 2.0 cents mean error (max 6.8), second
  2.7 (max 9.5), all within 10 cents; widths 0.85–1.16 of the reference
  (10–90%). The alternates: forked Eb4 −2/−12, side Bb4 +1/+1. The
  rendered lattice over 15 fingerings (`passive`): 3.0 cents mean, 97%
  within 10. The altissimo's first-to-third peak ratio is within 6% of
  the reference from C#6 to D#6, and 9% and 14% high at E6 and F6.
- **Untuned pitch.** Against the reference peaks less their mean offset
  (32 cents, the reed and player), the fingerings' own pitches differ by
  12 cents rms. The throat Bb4 is the outlier: in tune when soft but about
  40 cents flat from mf up.
- **Tuning.** All 38 notes and the three alternates are within 3.9 cents
  at pressures 0.3, 0.45 and 0.6 (`check`). Quarter tones are within
  0.5 cents on average and 2.2 at worst (`quarters`).
- **Slurs.** The grid covers 9 slurs across and within the registers, at
  pressures 0.3 and 0.55 and `key_time` 0, 0.4 and 1.0 (`slurs`). Every
  slur keeps the twelfth below at −13 dB or less and its third partial
  within 6.3 dB of its steady level. Within a register and descending,
  slurs settle in 2–25 ms (42 ms for a soft, sloppy D5–G4) with no dip.
  Ascents across the break settle in 25–35 ms and dip 3–6 dB.
- **Level.** At a fixed pressure the registers' mean levels rise about
  4 dB from the chalumeau to the clarion and altissimo.
- **Cost.** One voice costs 142 megacycles per second over the 4 s of
  `woodwind_callback` on `lattice-slurs.muz` and 253 at the 99th
  percentile (compiled), 3.6% and 6.3% of a 4 GHz core. It uses 523 of
  its 1,024 nodes (`muz check` counts them).

```sh
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py report    # fitted model vs reference
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py passive   # rendered lattice vs reference
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py tune      # each fingering's own pitch, refined
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py quarters  # quarter tones folded into the shading
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py check     # tuning at three dynamics
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py slurs     # the slur grid
```

### Remaining limits

- **Ascents across the break are a new attack.** Written A4–B4 and G4–D5
  settle in 25–35 ms and dip 3–6 dB (most at pressure 0.3 and `key_time`
  1), against 2–25 ms elsewhere. The cause is in the bore, not the keys:
  as the left hand seals over the open throat key, the throat note's
  resonance does not turn into the clarion's. It slides down toward the
  long tube's first mode (A4: from 422 Hz with the register key open to
  396 Hz, then 308 Hz once the fingers seal), the reed follows it, and the
  clarion resonance (448 Hz) comes down from above as a second branch.
  Every order of the keys has this crossing, so the clarion grows anew,
  at a rate its Q and the reed's gain at soft dynamics set (about 20 ms
  per neper at pressure 0.3). Tried in this round, each within 10% of the
  grid or worse: any order and speed of the thumb, register key, A key
  and left hand; finger curtains; a lighter or firmer clarion lip; more
  breath on the low clarion; the register's jet resistance at 0–8 times;
  a voicing transient from 0 to 30 tract impedances, 15–80 ms. A strong
  tract pulls the pitch onto the clarion sooner but takes level while it
  lasts. Leading with the register key (`vented`, 15–100 ms, a quarter
  to the whole lift) moves the grid by under 1 ms and deepens the dips on
  this geometry, so its lift is 0 here. On the profiled bore below, with
  open G and G# played little finger up, the same ascents settle in at most
  35 ms with every dip under 3 dB, and with the register key leading by
  35 ms in at most 30 ms (mean 26) and dips under 2.7 dB.
- **Throat Bb4 is flat from mf up** by about 40 cents untuned (−7 cents at
  pressure 0.3, −41 at 0.45 and 0.6), and the lip corrects it. Its
  fingering matches UNSW's chart (A and G# keys, register key, thumb hole
  open, right hand down) and its first two impedance peaks are within
  1.5 cents of UNSW's. Two measured properties pull it down as it gets
  louder: UNSW's own second peak is 104 cents below three times the first
  (A4's is 48 below), so its third harmonic falls on the flank of that
  peak; and the register tube's jet resistance at the literature's
  strength reaches about 0.65 bore impedances at mf (0.44 at pressure 0.3,
  0.75 at 0.6, read with the `register` probe), a quarter of the tube's
  reactance. Without the jet the pitch still falls 36 cents from pressure
  0.3 to 0.6; the side-key
  Bb4, with the same first peak and a second peak where the third harmonic
  lands, holds within 6. A refit with the real tube (1.47 mm, 13 mm,
  projecting about 2 mm) matches the reference as well and plays the
  same. No geometry or fingering error was found, and players report the
  same tendency: the clarinet goes flat when played loud and sharp when
  soft, players compensate with lip pressure (Schatt, Woodwind Doubling
  Resource Project, "Intonation"), and the throat tones move most (they
  fall twice as far as the rest when the barrel is pulled, R Clarinet
  Products, "Tuning the clarinet for performance"). No source gives cents
  against dynamic; the lip corrects it here as a player does.
- **E6 and F6 have a weak third peak** (9% and 14% against the first). A
  full refit with the bore profile fits them within 1–3%, and all third
  peaks to 30 cents on average: a narrower mouthpiece chamber
  (6.75 mm radius over 40 mm, the tone chamber under the baffle), a
  slightly reverse-tapered upper joint (7.23 to 7.20 mm), a widening lower
  joint (7.84 mm) and a flare to 24 mm. But that refit moves the hole
  geometry, and the soft B4 then falls into the low register or a
  multiphonic after a slur, and open G with the little finger down splits
  its second resonance in two (playing open G and G# little finger up,
  `LITTLE_UP`, avoids it). With that, this profiled bore holds the slur
  grid with no stray modes, plays the forked Bb5 within 0.1 cents with
  its twelfth below at −85 dB or less, and fixes the ascents (above). It
  is still held back: the soft F#5 and E6 do not speak reliably (at
  pressure 0.3 F#5 sounds +14 cents with its twelfth below at −24 dB, and
  is silent in some contexts; E6 is 16 cents flat). Its fourth impedance
  peaks, which the fit does not see, are two to three times the
  reference's near twice F#5 and G5 (8.4 against 4.0 at 1384 Hz), and its
  clarion peaks are 10% lower than the committed geometry's. A refit with
  those peaks in its residuals is the next step (`fit --stage profile` or
  `--stage all` with `--third 0.3` fits the profile). The profiled bore is
  `clarinet-geometry-profiled.json`: to play it, copy it over
  `clarinet-geometry.json`, set `JET_K = 0.6` (its register tube is the
  real one) and `LITTLE_UP = True` in the tool, run `muz`, `tune` and
  `quarters`, and set `vent_crack = 1` in `lattice-clarinet.muz`.
- **The forked Bb5 wanders at p** (about 25 cents) and shows its twelfth
  below at forte (−28 to −37 dB) on this geometry: its first impedance
  peak stands at 1.47 times its second (the standard Bb5's at 1.29), so
  the low mode competes. UNSW did not measure it. The profiled bore plays
  it cleanly (above).
- **The reed's closing pressure rises with lip force** here
  (K H = (1 + 2.25 F)(1 − 0.4 F)), while Almeida et al.'s extinction line
  falls about 3.3 kPa per newton (an effective area of 3 cm²). Their
  lighter clarion force and Fritz & Wolfe's steady tract wait on a reed
  whose stiffness, opening and mass follow the lip as measured.
- **No forked Bb3/F5.** Boehm charts list none; the only fork the lattice
  can finger is 40 cents flat, so it is left out.
- **The player model is partly measured.** The chalumeau's lip force is
  Almeida et al.'s (2013); the clarion's and altissimo's forces, the
  crescendo slope, the vocal tract's strength per note and the voicing on
  ascents are estimated within the literature's ranges and were set by
  playing the slur grid and the range. Measured alternatives broke the
  instrument here. With a tract fixed at 1.2 kHz and 1.3–2.5 bore
  impedances, as Fritz & Wolfe (2005) measured in normal playing, the
  clarion at Almeida et al.'s lighter G5 force (0.65 N) stops or squeaks
  at forte (B4, G5, B5), and at this reed's 1.14 N a sloppy A4–B4 slur at
  mf can land in the low register. A smooth power law in frequency for
  the tract (1 at 466 Hz to 6 at 932 Hz) leaves D5 weaker than the table
  and makes the soft, sloppy D5–G4 slur fall out of G4.
- **Third impedance peaks** are 43 cents off on average, most of it the
  throat notes' (+120–250 cents): the fitted holes' chimneys sit at their
  4 mm bound, which sets the open-hole cutoff too high.

`reed_dynamics.muz` also provides the earlier bandpass correction to a
quasi-static reed (`single`) and an optional upstream resonance
(`voicing`). Both reeds accept the vocal tract. `reed-voicing.muz`
demonstrates them.

## Passive measurements

The passive examples inject a broadband pulse at the reed plane with the
exciter removed. Measure reflection, normalized input impedance, and
radiation with the same tool:

```sh
uv run tools/woodwind-passive.py examples/woodwind-prototypes/passive-cylinder.muz --radiated
uv run tools/woodwind-passive.py examples/woodwind-prototypes/passive-clarinet.muz --track g4 --min-hz 120 --radiated
```

The cylinder is a reference case with a 2 ms round trip and a first
impedance peak near 250 Hz. `passive-clarinet.muz` holds 15 fingerings
of the lattice clarinet, one track per written note. It is small-signal,
with no jet resistance, as the reference was measured. These results
describe held fingerings at the requested sample rate.
