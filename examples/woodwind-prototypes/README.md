# Fixed-bore clarinet experiments

## Two fingerings

`fixed-bore.muz` uses one nonlinear reed with three bidirectional delay
segments and two junctions. The register vent is a filtered local pressure
release. The G tone hole blends the local scattering between bore
transmission and reflection from an open end. The experiment has two
fingerings, concert A4 and F4, and tests the register transition with a
fixed bore:

```sh
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/register-slurs.muz \
  -o out/register-slurs.wav
```

## Tone-hole lattice clarinet

`clarinet_bore.muz` is a fixed-geometry B-flat clarinet, written E3–F6
(sounding MIDI 50–87). `lattice-clarinet.muz` plays it with the
`std/woodwinds` player's breath and articulation and a player model of its
own. Its parts:

- **Bore.** Cylinder segments between 20 holes (17 tone holes, two ring
  vents and the register hole) and a bell, as cubic delay lines, with a
  loss shelf on the mouthpiece segment and a two-pole bell reflection. Wall
  loss is lumped onto every fifth segment (each segment loses under 0.5%).
  Every segment stays live: an open hole does not cut off the bore below
  it.
- **Holes.** Each hole is a shunt junction with pressure
  `p = (1 - H_open)(1 - H_closed)(a + b)`. The open path is an inertance
  with a viscous resistance (narrower holes are lossier), a one-pole
  release whose corner and depth follow the open path's admittance
  `g(x)` at the key's travel. A pad and a finger over its hole both act as
  a curtain, `g = (1 + q) x / (x + q)`, so most of the change happens near
  the seat. The closed chimney is a compliance. The register hole also has
  a jet resistance growing with its flow (Atig, Dalmont & Gilbert 2004).
- **Geometry.** `clarinet_geometry.muz` is generated by
  `tools/clarinet-lattice.py fit` from `clarinet-geometry.json`. The tool
  fits hole positions, radii, chimneys and losses to the measured
  impedance peaks of a real B-flat clarinet, UNSW's `ImpedClarinet.xlsx`
  (Dickens, Smith & Wolfe), reduced to `clarinet-reference.json`. Its
  frequency-domain model is the discrete-time system the renderer runs
  (third-order Lagrange delays, prewarped trapezoidal one-poles at
  96 kHz), so the rendered lattice matches the fit. Two ring vents follow
  a measured Yamaha YCL-250 (Moers & Kergomard 2011): `v`, open only with
  the thumb and LH1 both up (open G), and the sliver `sl` above LH2. The
  result is an effective geometry, not a drawing.
- **Fingerings.** Each written note maps to its open holes, checked
  against UNSW's fingering diagrams and Boehm charts: chalumeau, the
  clarion a twelfth up with the register key, the altissimo on the fifth
  harmonic with LH1 vented (and the G#/D# key from D6 up). The throat notes
  are played with the right hand down, as players do to cross the break.
  Alternates (`fingering` control): the forked Eb4/Bb5 and the throat Bb4
  with the A key and side trill key. Quarter tones shade the changing holes
  to the fraction `shade` that sounds between the semitones
  (`tools/clarinet-lattice.py quarters` folds the played error into it).
  Across the break and into the altissimo the fingering changes just below
  the quarter tone and the player bends the upper fingering down.
- **Keys.** A finger or pad leaves its stop at rest and lands on its seat
  or stop at speed: the travel is a slew toward a target past either end,
  read through curves that hold their end values, so each motion ends at a
  definite moment and its filters rest. A finger moves in 5 ms and a spring
  in 8 ms from the note's change (Almeida, Chow, Smith & Wolfe 2009
  measured about 10 and 16 ms whole motions on the flute; the note's
  change already includes the player's anticipation). Fingers arrive in a
  fixed order (thumb and register key, left hand, right hand, little
  fingers), `stagger` times the note's `key_time` (0–1, default 0.4)
  apart.
- **Reed and lip.** The reed is `reed_dynamics.dynamic`, second order,
  its flow solved in closed form. One lip force F (0–1) sets it: a firmer
  lip closes the rest opening H and stiffens the reed K, so its closing
  pressure KH rises, zeta (as sqrt(H/K)) falls, and its resonance
  (1.5–2.7 kHz) and damping (q 0.15–0.45) rise. These ranges follow
  Tonetti & Piana (2025, reed resonance and damping against lip load),
  Almeida et al. (2013, squeaks near 1.0–1.2 kHz bound the lip-loaded
  resonance) and Szwarcberg et al. (q at least 0.2, zeta 0.05–0.4). The
  force is 0.8 at mf, lighter in the clarion (Almeida et al.: G5 plays at
  lower force than G4) and lighter when louder (players loosen to play
  louder, Fuks & Sundberg 1999).
- **Breath.** Blowing pressure falls about 8% per octave up the range and
  rises 15% on the lowest clarion notes, after Fuks & Sundberg's measured
  arpeggios. Breath turbulence is a noise flow at the reed channel, so it
  radiates through the bore.
- **Vocal tract.** One resonance upstream of the reed, about 1 bore
  characteristic impedance in the chalumeau rising to 6 in the upper
  clarion and 10 in the altissimo, 150 Hz (altissimo 120 Hz) above the
  note but not below 600 Hz (Chen, Smith & Wolfe 2009; Pàmies-Vilà et al.
  2020 found tract adjustments in a fraction of clarion notes and most
  altissimo ones). On a slur up into a higher register, and on a tongued
  attack in the clarion or altissimo, the player voices the new note: for
  40 ms the resonance moves onto it, 10 stronger. The `voicing` control adds
  up to 8.
- **Tuning.** The player keeps the note in tune with a variable mouthpiece
  length, from the tuned frequency against the fingering's own pitch at
  the current dynamic (`tools/clarinet-lattice.py tune`, with two passes
  that fold the residual into `clarinet_tuning.muz`), between 70 cents down
  and 50 up.
- **Radiation.** Each open hole and the bell radiate as monopoles: the
  far-field pressure is the time derivative of their summed volume flow
  (an open hole's flow is twice its pressure release; the bell's the
  incident less the reflected wave). Their spacing and the bell's
  directivity are left out.

Render the slurs, a phrase, the phrase on both clarinets, and the range
with alternates, quarter tones and a bend:

```sh
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/lattice-slurs.muz -o out/lattice-slurs.wav
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/lattice-phrase.muz -o out/lattice-phrase.wav
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/blend-phrase.muz -o out/blend-phrase.wav
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/lattice-fingerings.muz -o out/lattice-fingerings.wav
```

### Measurements (2026-11-14)

- **Passive impedance.** The fit against the reference over the 38
  standard fingerings: first peak 2.0 cents mean error (max 6.8), second
  2.7 (max 9.5), all within 10 cents; widths 0.85–1.16 of the reference
  (10–90%). The alternates: forked Eb4 −2/−12, side Bb4 +1/+1. The
  rendered lattice over 15 fingerings (`passive`): 3.0 cents mean, 97%
  within 10. The altissimo's first-to-third peak ratio is within 4% of
  the reference from C#6 to D#6, and 13–19% high at E6 and F6.
- **Untuned pitch.** Against the reference peaks less their mean offset
  (32 cents, the reed and player), the fingerings' own pitches differ by
  12 cents rms. The throat Bb4 is the outlier: in tune when soft but about
  40 cents flat from mf up.
- **Tuning.** All 38 notes and the three alternates are within 3.6 cents
  at pressures 0.3, 0.45 and 0.6 (`check`). Quarter tones are within
  2 cents on average and 10 at worst (`quarters`).
- **Slurs.** The grid covers 9 slurs across and within the registers, at
  pressures 0.3 and 0.55 and `key_time` 0, 0.4 and 1.0 (`slurs`). Every
  slur keeps the twelfth below at −13 dB or less and its third partial
  within 6.3 dB of its steady level. Within a register and descending,
  slurs settle in 2–25 ms (40 ms for a soft, sloppy D5–G4) with no dip.
  Ascents across the break settle in 25–38 ms and dip 3–7 dB.
- **Level.** At a fixed pressure the registers' mean levels rise about
  4 dB from the chalumeau to the clarion and altissimo.
- **Cost.** One voice costs 192 megacycles per second over the 4 s of
  `woodwind_callback` on `lattice-slurs.muz` and 413 at the 99th
  percentile (compiled; 419 and 917 interpreted), 4.8% and 10% of a 4 GHz
  core. It uses close to 512 nodes.

```sh
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py report    # fitted model vs reference
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py passive   # rendered lattice vs reference
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py tune      # each fingering's own pitch, refined
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py quarters  # quarter tones folded into the shading
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py check     # tuning at three dynamics
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py slurs     # the slur grid
```

### Remaining limits

- **Ascents across the break are a new attack.** Written A4–B4 and
  G4–D5 settle in 25–38 ms and dip 3–7 dB (most at pressure 0.3 and
  `key_time` 1), against 2–25 ms elsewhere. They no longer pass through
  the low register.
- **Throat Bb4 is flat from mf up** by about 40 cents untuned, and the
  lip correction reaches its 50-cent limit there.
- **E6 and F6 have a weak third peak,** probably for want of the lower
  joint's and bell's flare; the vocal tract carries them.
- **No forked Bb3/F5.** Boehm charts list none; the only fork the lattice
  can finger is 40 cents flat, so it is left out.
- **The player model's settings are chosen within the literature's
  ranges, not measured on one player.** The lip force per register, the
  tract strength per register and the voicing on ascents were set by
  playing the slur grid and the range.
- **Third impedance peaks** are 43 cents off on average: the lattice has no
  bore flare or dispersion.
- **No std version yet.** The voice is at the node limit.

`reed_dynamics.muz` also provides the earlier bandpass correction to a
quasi-static reed (`single`) and an optional upstream resonance
(`voicing`). Both reeds accept the vocal tract. `reed-voicing.muz`
demonstrates them.

## Passive measurements

The passive examples inject a broadband pulse at the reed plane with the
exciter removed. Measure reflection, normalized input impedance, and
radiation with the same tool:

```sh
uv run tools/woodwind-passive.py examples/woodwind-prototypes/passive-cylinder.muz --radiated
uv run tools/woodwind-passive.py examples/woodwind-prototypes/passive-clarinet.muz --track g4 --min-hz 120 --radiated
```

The cylinder is a reference case with a 2 ms round trip and a first
impedance peak near 250 Hz. `passive-clarinet.muz` holds 15 fingerings
of the lattice clarinet, one track per written note. It is small-signal,
with no jet resistance, as the reference was measured. These results
describe held fingerings at the requested sample rate.
