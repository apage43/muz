# Fixed-bore clarinet experiments

## Two fingerings

`fixed-bore.muz` uses one nonlinear reed with three bidirectional delay
segments and two junctions. The register vent is a filtered local pressure
release. The G tone hole blends the local scattering between bore
transmission and reflection from an open end. The experiment has two
fingerings, concert A4 and F4, and tests the register transition with a
fixed bore:

```sh
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/register-slurs.muz \
  -o out/register-slurs.wav
```

## Tone-hole lattice clarinet

`clarinet_bore.muz` is a fixed-geometry B-flat clarinet, written E3–F6
(sounding MIDI 50–87). `lattice-clarinet.muz` plays it with the
`std/woodwinds` player. Its parts:

- **Bore.** Cylinder segments between 18 holes (17 tone holes and the
  register hole) and a bell, as cubic delay lines with a round-trip wall
  loss per segment, a loss shelf on the mouthpiece segment, and a two-pole
  bell reflection. Every segment stays live: an open hole does not cut off
  the bore below it.
- **Holes.** Each hole is a shunt junction with pressure
  `p = (1 - H_open)(1 - H_closed)(a + b)`. The open path is an inertance
  with a resistance, a one-pole release whose corner and depth follow the
  key's travel through the open path's admittance `g(x)`. Keyed holes use
  a pad's curtain inertance, `g = (1 + q) x / (x + q)`, and finger holes
  use `g = x`. The closed chimney is a compliance, a highpass whose gain
  keeps its low-frequency slope. The series inertance shortens the
  segments. All elements are lumped filters, with no delay line inside a
  chimney. The register hole also has a nonlinear (jet) resistance growing
  with its flow (Atig, Dalmont & Gilbert 2004), which damps the low
  register under the clarion at playing amplitudes.
- **Geometry.** `clarinet_geometry.muz` is generated by
  `tools/clarinet-lattice.py fit` from `clarinet-geometry.json`. The tool
  fits hole positions, radii, chimney heights and losses to measured
  impedance peaks of a real B-flat clarinet: UNSW's `ImpedClarinet.xlsx`
  (Dickens, Smith & Wolfe), reduced to `clarinet-reference.json`. The
  result is an effective geometry, not a drawing. Several chimneys sit at
  the 2.5 mm bound.
- **Fingerings.** Each written note maps to the set of open holes, with
  the register key as one of them: Boehm chalumeau fingerings, the clarion
  a twelfth up with the register key, the altissimo on the fifth harmonic
  with LH1 vented, and the F#4 cross fingering. A note's `fingering`
  control picks an alternate: the forked Eb4 and Bb5, and the throat Bb4
  with the side trill key. Between two semitones a changing hole moves
  through the travel that halves its admittance, so a quarter tone is a
  half hole. Tuning comes from geometry and fingering only.
- **Keys.** Every hole has its own opening and closing time constant
  (finger, spring-closed key, spring-open key, register key). Fingers
  move in a fixed order: the thumb and register key, then the left hand,
  the right hand and the little fingers. Each step is `stagger` times
  the note's `key_time` control (0–1, default 0.4) apart.
- **Reed and player.** The reed is `reed_dynamics.dynamic`, a
  second-order reed with its flow solved in closed form. Its resonance and
  lip damping follow the register (`embouchure`), because the upper
  clarion and altissimo only speak with a lower, less damped reed. The
  player keeps the note in tune with a variable mouthpiece length. That
  length comes from the tuned frequency (tuning, glide, drift, vibrato)
  against the fingering's own pitch at the current dynamic, measured by
  `tools/clarinet-lattice.py tune` into `clarinet_tuning.muz`, within
  ±50 cents.

Render the slurs, a phrase on both clarinets, and the range with
alternates, quarter tones and a bend:

```sh
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/lattice-slurs.muz -o out/lattice-slurs.wav
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/lattice-phrase.muz -o out/lattice-phrase.wav
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/blend-phrase.muz -o out/blend-phrase.wav
target/release/muz render examples/woodwind-prototypes/lattice-fingerings.muz -o out/lattice-fingerings.wav
```

### Measurements (2026-11-14)

- **Passive impedance.** Fitted model against the reference over all 40
  fingerings: first peak 7.2 cents mean error, 76% within 10 cents; second
  peak 5.7 cents mean error, 87% within 10 cents. Peak widths median 0.97
  of the reference (10–90%: 0.72–1.23). The rendered lattice over 15
  representative fingerings (`passive`) is 11.3 cents mean error, 47%
  within 10 cents. Discretization adds about 5–10 cents to second peaks.
  The worst fingerings are G4 (−32 and −47 cents) and F#4 (+33): the
  fitted thumb hole cannot serve the open G and the cross fingering at
  once.
- **Tuning.** All 38 notes and the three alternates are within 4.0 cents
  (mean 1.7) at pressures 0.3, 0.45 and 0.6. They are within 3.7 cents at
  0.38 and 0.52, which were not calibrated. The untuned fingerings span
  −49 cents (throat Bb4) to +22 cents.
- **Slurs.** The grid covers 9 slurs across and within the registers, at
  pressures 0.3 and 0.55 and `key_time` 0.4 and 1.0 (`slurs`). Most slurs
  settle within 10–28 ms, with no dip and no stray mode: the twelfth
  below stays at −17 dB or less, and third-partial bursts stay within
  4 dB.
- **Cost.** 461 nodes per voice, against the 512 limit. One voice costs
  585 megacycles per second of audio on average and 975 at the 99th
  percentile (`woodwind_callback` on `lattice-slurs.muz`), 15% and 24% of
  a 4 GHz core. A held `std/woodwinds` clarinet costs 185.

```sh
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py report    # fitted model vs reference
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py passive   # rendered lattice vs reference
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py check     # tuning at three dynamics
uv run tools/clarinet-lattice.py slurs     # the slur grid
```

### Remaining limits

- **Ascending slurs across the break grunt.** Written A4–B4 and G4–D5
  pass through the low register for about 40 ms, 6–13 dB under the new
  note, and the level dips up to 9 dB. They settle in 45–63 ms.
- **Soft descent to the open G cracks.** Written D5–G4 at pressure 0.3
  with the default key timing releases the register key too late, and
  the note locks into its twelfth.
- **The embouchure schedule and lip correction are fitted, not
  measured.** They hide fingerings whose own pitch is up to 49 cents off.
  The upper clarion and altissimo depend on a reed resonance of
  1.2–1.5 kHz.
- **Alternates.** The forked Bb5 can fall to its undertone on a tongued
  attack. The forked Bb3/F5 was removed: modeled as written, it is 90
  cents sharp.
- **Quarter tones.** They come out about 20 cents sharp of the half-hole
  ideal. A quarter tone across the break snaps to one side.
- **Level.** The altissimo is about 10 dB louder than the chalumeau. The
  output is a highpassed sum of hole and bell flows plus mouthpiece
  pressure, not a radiation model.
- **No std version yet.** One voice costs 2.4 times the std clarinet and
  is close to the node limit.

`reed_dynamics.muz` also provides the earlier bandpass correction to a
quasi-static reed (`single`) and an optional upstream resonance
(`voicing`). Both reeds accept the vocal tract. `reed-voicing.muz`
demonstrates them.

## Passive measurements

The passive examples inject a broadband pulse at the reed plane with the
exciter removed. Measure reflection, normalized input impedance, and
radiation with the same tool:

```sh
uv run tools/woodwind-passive.py examples/woodwind-prototypes/passive-cylinder.muz --radiated
uv run tools/woodwind-passive.py examples/woodwind-prototypes/passive-clarinet.muz --track g4 --min-hz 120 --radiated
```

The cylinder is a reference case with a 2 ms round trip and a first
impedance peak near 250 Hz. `passive-clarinet.muz` holds 15 fingerings
of the lattice clarinet, one track per written note. It is small-signal,
with no jet resistance, as the reference was measured. These results
describe held fingerings at the requested sample rate.
